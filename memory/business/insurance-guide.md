# Insurance Requirements Guide — Irvin's Tech & Electrical Services

**Business Type:** Cybersecurity, IT Services, Electrical, Computer Repair, MSP/MSSP  
**Prepared for:** Irvin Avitia

---

## 1. General Liability Insurance

**What it covers:** Third-party bodily injury, property damage, and advertising injury.

**Why you need it:** Standard requirement for any business interacting with clients on-site. Covers accidents like a client tripping over equipment, or accidentally damaging someone's property during a service call.

**Recommended coverage:** **$1–$2 million per occurrence**  
**Aggregate limit:** **$2 million**

*Typical cost for small IT/electrical biz:* $400–$800/year

---

## 2. Professional Liability (E&O) Insurance

**What it covers:** Errors, omissions, negligence in professional services. Also covers defense costs if a client claims your advice or work caused them financial harm.

**Why you need it:** Your core value is cybersecurity and technical expertise. If a client claims your configuration caused a breach, or your advice led to downtime/loss — this covers it. Not having E&O is a dealbreaker for most B2B contracts.

**Recommended coverage:** **$1–$2 million**  
*Push for $2M if handling enterprise clients or anything critical infrastructure.*

*Typical cost:* $800–$2,000/year depending on revenue and risk profile

---

## 3. Workers' Compensation Insurance

**What it covers:** Medical costs, lost wages, and disability if an employee gets injured or sick on the job. Also protects you from lawsuits by employees.

**Why you need it:** **Required by law in most states** if you have employees (even part-time). California (where you are) mandates it. Covers electrical work hazards especially.

**Recommended coverage:**  
- **Statutory limits** per California requirements  
- **Employers Liability:** $1 million per accident

*Typical cost:* Varies wildly by payroll — budget ~$1,500–$4,000/year for a small team

---

## 4. Cyber Insurance

**What it covers:** First-party costs (ransomware, data recovery, breach response) and third-party claims (client data leaks, regulatory fines, lawsuits).

**Why you need it:** You handle cybersecurity for clients — irony if you don't have your own. Covers: incident response, legal fees, notification costs, business interruption, extortion.

**Recommended coverage:** **$1–$2 million**  
*Get a policy that covers: ransomware, forensic investigation, legal defense, regulatory fines.*

*Typical cost:* $1,000–$3,000/year for small biz

---

## 5. Coverage Summary

| Insurance Type | Min Recommended | Optimal |
|----------------|------------------|---------|
| General Liability | $1M | $2M |
| Professional Liability (E&O) | $1M | $2M |
| Workers' Comp | Statutory | Statutory + $1M Employers Liability |
| Cyber Insurance | $1M | $2M |

**Total estimated annual premium:** $3,700–$9,800/year (varies by payroll, revenue, claims history)

---

## Quick Recommendations

1. **Bundle General + Cyber** — many insurers give a multi-policy discount
2. **Get E&O BEFORE signing any MSA** — most enterprise clients will require a certificate of insurance (COI)
3. **Check your lease/contract requirements** — landlord or client contracts often specify minimums
4. **Consider BOP (Business Owner's Policy)** — packages general liability + property at better rates
