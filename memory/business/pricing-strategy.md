# Competitive Pricing Strategy

*ElectricPlanner — Irvin's Tech & Electrical Services*  
*Effective: March 2026*

---

## Executive Summary

Irvin's business has three distinct competitive advantages that justify **premium pricing** in most scenarios:

1. **Northrop Grumman cybersecurity credibility** — Enterprise-grade expertise competitors can't match
2. **Board-level repair + micro soldering** — Near-zero local competition
3. **IT + Electrical combo** — Single-throat-to-choke, no other vendor offers this

Don't compete on price. Compete on value.

---

## 1. How to Price Against Competitors

### The Verdict: **Premium Positioning** (with strategic exceptions)

| Scenario | Strategy | Rationale |
|----------|----------|-----------|
| **General MSP services** | Match or slight premium (5-15% above market) | You're not commoditized — you're specialized |
| **Board-level repair** | **Premium (20-40% above)** | Unique capability, no local competition |
| **Security assessments** | **Premium (20-50% above)** | Defense contractor creds = enterprise quality |
| **Electrical + IT bundles** | **Premium (10-20% above)** | Convenience factor, single vendor |
| ** commodity services (basic helpdesk, simple installs)** | Match or undercut slightly | Price-sensitive clients exist; win them on upsell |
| **New client acquisition** | Competitive (match market) | Get foot in door, then demonstrate value |
| **Retainer clients** | Premium justified | Lock in recurring revenue at higher margin |

### Why Not Undercut?

- **Undercutting signals cheap** — "You get what you pay for"
- **Attracts price-shoppers** — Worst clients, hardest to satisfy
- **Unsustainable** — You'll chase competitors down a race to the bottom
- **Devalues your expertise** — 20+ years of experience shouldn't be discounted

### When Undercutting Makes Sense

1. **Strategic loss leaders** — Break-even or slight loss to land a major account
2. **Block competitive threat** — If a competitor is aggressively courting a key prospect
3. **Capacity fill** — Slow periods; better utilized than empty calendar
4. **Educational/non-profit** — Goodwill, community positioning

**Rule:** Never undercut on services where you're clearly the best option.

## 2. When to Discount, When Not To

### ✅ Discounts That Work

| Discount Type | When | How Much |
|---------------|------|----------|
| **Annual prepayment** | Client wants monthly, you want cash flow | 10-15% (sweet spot) |
| **Multi-year contract** | Lock in long-term revenue | 15-20% for 3-year |
| **Bundled services** | IT + electrical, or multiple services | 10-15% (but you're still winning) |
| **Referral bonus** | Existing client refers new business | $100-250 credit or service credit |
| **Non-profit/charity** | Aligns with community goals | Case-by-case (usually 10-20%) |
| **First project discount** | New client, demo your value | 5-10% — don't give away the farm |
| **Repeat client** | Loyalty reward | 5-10% — but only after 2+ paid projects |

### ❌ Discounts That Don't

| Discount Type | Why Not |
|---------------|---------|
| **"Just because you asked"** | Trains clients to negotiate |
| **Percentage off hourly rate** | Undermines value perception |
| **Unbundling to discount** | Never discount line items; discount total |
| **Emergency/after-hours** | Should be 1.5x, not discounted |
| **Rush jobs** | Premium, not discount |
| **Competition beat** | Don't get in a bidding war |

### The Discount Framework

**Always require something in return:**
- "I can do 10% off if you sign a 12-month agreement"
- "15% off for annual prepayment — saves us both admin overhead"
- "I'll bundle these three services — gives you 12% off the total"

**Never discount without asking for:**
- Commitment (contract length)
- Scope expansion (add more services)
- Referral (introduce you to someone)
- Testimonial/review

## 3. Value-Based Pricing

### What Is Value-Based Pricing?

Price based on the **outcome for the client**, not the **cost to you**.

| Service | Cost-Based (Wrong) | Value-Based (Right) |
|---------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Board repair | $150 labor + parts | "This board costs $800 new. I fix it for $400. You save $400." |
| Security assessment | $150/hr × 20 hours | "A breach costs you $50k+ in downtime, data loss, reputational damage. This assessment prevents that." |
| MSP retainer | $100/user/mo | "$100/user/mo = $2/cup of coffee. Meanwhile, I prevent $10k in downtime." |
| Electrical panel | $1,500 job cost | "This panel upgrade prevents a fire that would cost $50k in damage + insurance implications." |

### Irvin's Value Pricing by Service

#### Managed IT Services (MSP)

| Tier | Price | Value Pitch |
|------|-------|-------------|
| **Essential** (under 25 users) | $125-150/user/mo | "You get a vCIO, proactive monitoring, and a guy who actually answers the phone. No more 'who do we call?'" |
| **Professional** (25-75 users) | $100-125/user/mo | "Full managed services + security stack. Break-fix is included — we fix it before you know there's a problem." |
| **Enterprise** (75+ users) | $75-100/user/mo | "Dedicated team, compliance management, 24/7 monitoring. We'll run your IT like a defense contractor." |

#### Project/Hourly Work

| Service | Rate | Value Positioning |
|---------|------|-------------------|
| General IT consulting | $150-175/hr | "I'm former Northrop Grumman. You get defense-level thinking at local rates." |
| Cybersecurity assessment | $3,000-8,000/project | "I find the holes before the bad guys do. A breach costs $50k+ minimum." |
| vCISO | $750-1,500/mo | "Fractional CISO access. Someone who built security programs at defense contractors." |
| Board-level repair | $200-400/job | "Most shops replace. I repair. You save 50-70% vs. new board or replacement." |
| Electrical (labor) | $95-125/hr | "Licensed, insured, code-compliant. And I understand your network infrastructure." |

#### Electrical + IT Bundles

The combo is your **unfair advantage**. Price it to capture the full value:

| Bundle | Price | Value |
|--------|-------|-------|
| "Office Starter" (8 drops + 4 outlets + UPS) | $2,500-3,000 | "One company. One invoice. Done." |
| "Shop Ready" (electrical + network) | $3,500-4,500 | "Your workshop, powered and connected." |
| "Server Room Ready" | $5,500-7,000 | "From power to rack — I spec'd it, I built it, I support it." |

## 4. Pricing Psychology & Closing

### Anchor High

- **Present premium first** — "Our comprehensive package is $X"
- **Then offer "standard"** — "If that's outside budget, we also have a standard option at $Y"
- Never present only the cheap option

### Package, Don't Unbundle

- **Don't** — "It's $100/hr, but I'll give you a deal"
- **Do** — "This project is $2,400 — includes everything we discussed"

### Use Anchoring Numbers

- "$200/month for enterprise-grade IT protection"
- "$3,000 to find vulnerabilities before they find you"
- "$400 to fix what would cost $1,200 to replace"

### The Close

When client hesitates on price:
1. **Confirm concern** — "Is it the budget, or is there something I missed?"
2. **Reframe** — "Let me ask — what would it cost if this breaks and you're down for 2 days?"
3. **Offer an alternative** — "We could phase this — do phase 1 now, phase 2 next quarter"
4. **Never discount immediately** — Pause, let it sit, then offer concession if warranted

## 5. Quick Reference: Pricing Decision Tree

```
Is this a commodity service (basic install, helpdesk, simple repair)?
├── YES → Match market rate (or undercut slightly to win)
└── NO → Continue

Does Irvin have a unique differentiator?
├── YES (board repair, cybersecurity, IT+electrical combo)
│   └── → Premium pricing (10-40% above market)
└── NO → Match market

Is the client:
├── Long-term/retainer potential?
│   └── → Slight discount for commitment (10-15% off annual)
├── Price-shopping/will-haggle?
│   └── → Hold firm; not your client
├── Enterprise/complex?
│   └── → Premium justified; they expect it
└── Small/price-sensitive?
    └── → Offer entry-tier or bundle to increase perceived value
```

## Summary

| Principle | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| **Don't undercut** | You're not cheap. You're specialized. |

| **Price the value** | Outcome-based, not time-based |
| **Bundle aggressively** | IT + electrical = premium positioning |
| **Anchor high** | Present premium first, then "value" option |
