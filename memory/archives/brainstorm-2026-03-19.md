## Idea 1: Board-Level Repair Training & Certification Program

**Description:**  
Your micro soldering and board-level repair skills are rare. Create a tiered training program (online + hands-on workshops) for repair shops, electronics companies, and makers. Certification earned. Recurring revenue from enrollments + corporate bulk licenses.

**Why it works:**
- High barrier to entry (few people can teach this)
- Complements existing repair business (generates referrals)
- Scalable (video content + occasional workshops)
- Premium pricing justified by expertise

**First Step:**  
Document 3-5 of your most common board-repair job types with before/after photos, tools used, and time investment. Create a simple 1-page curriculum outline and price research (check Udemy/Skillshare premium courses + hands-on workshop pricing). Target early audience: local repair shops needing upskilling.

## Idea 2: Cybersecurity Assessment SaaS Tool

**Description:**  
Leverage your Northrop-level cyber + DevOps expertise to build a lightweight SaaS that helps small businesses run self-service security assessments. Export PDF reports, identify gaps, recommend fixes. Subscription ($29-99/mo per company, or per-assessment pricing).

**Why it works:**
- Small businesses need security audits but can't afford $5k+
- You've done thousands of these—automate the questionnaire & scoring
- Recurring revenue with low marginal cost
- Can white-label for MSPs (B2B upsell)

**First Step:**  
Map out your typical cybersecurity assessment workflow (10-15 key questions/checks). Build a simple Google Form prototype or Notion template. Test with 3-5 current/past clients. Get feedback on gaps. Validate willingness to pay.

## Idea 3: IoT Device Security Integration Service

**Description:**  
Combine your electrical + cybersecurity expertise. Offer secure smart building/home installations (smart lighting, HVAC, security cameras, access control) with hardened networking and compliance (zero-trust architecture, segmentation, monitoring). Rare combo—most electricians don't understand cyber; most cyber folks don't do electrical.

**Why it works:**
- Growing market (smart buildings, industrial IoT)
- Huge security blind spot (electricians install; IT folks scramble later)
- Higher margins (integration + security consulting)
- Stickier than labor-only work (ongoing monitoring/updates)

**First Step:**  
Interview 3-5 of your existing electrical clients about smart device adoption plans. Ask: "What's your biggest concern installing smart systems?" Document pain points. Research 2-3 competitors in "secure smart building" space. Map out a 1-page service offering (assessment → design → install → 6-mo monitoring).
