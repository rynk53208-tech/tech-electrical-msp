Security Best Practices

🔑 Use Passphrases Over Passwords
Encourage users to think of memorable phrases rather than random characters. "BlueSky!Coffee@Sunrise" is stronger and more memorable than "B7d$Qk9m".
🚫 Never Share Passwords
Passwords are personal credentials. Even IT staff should not ask for passwords. Use session-based admin access instead.
🔐 Use Password Managers
Recommend enterprise password managers (Bitwarden, 1Password, LastPass) for secure storage of complex passwords across systems.
📱 Enable Multi-Factor Authentication
MFA adds a second layer of security. Require it for admin accounts, email, and critical systems. 90% of breaches could be prevented with MFA.
🔄 Silent Password Expiration (NIST 2017)
Old guidance (90-day rotation) is deprecated. NIST now recommends longer or no expiration, with breach-triggered resets instead.
⚠️ Avoid Common Requirements
Don't require excessive special characters or frequent changes—they lead to "Password123!" patterns and Post-it notes. Quality > quantity.
🛡️ Implement Account Lockout
Lock accounts after 5-6 failed login attempts for 30 minutes. This prevents brute-force attacks while staying user-friendly.
🔍 Monitor Compromised Passwords
Use "Have I Been Pwned" API checks during password resets to prevent users from setting previously breached passwords.
📋 Regular Audits
Quarterly reviews of failed login attempts, policy violations, and privilege accounts. Spot anomalies fast.
🎓 User Training
Annual security awareness training is the strongest defense. Phishing simulations are more effective than strict policies.
