Priority Definitions (P1–P4)

Priority Label Definition Business Impact Examples
P1 Critical Complete service outage or security breach affecting entire business operations. All users/sites down. Revenue directly impacted. Potential data loss or breach. Server down, ransomware attack, total network failure, electrical fault causing shutdown, POS system failure during peak hours
P2 High Major functionality impaired; significant performance degradation; no viable workaround. Large group of users impacted. Core business functions degraded. VPN inaccessible, email service down, backup failures, internet degraded >50%, critical device offline, HVAC/electrical affecting server room
P3 Medium Partial functionality loss; workaround available; non-critical system affected. Limited number of users impacted. Business continues with workaround. Single workstation failure, slow Wi-Fi in one area, printer issues, minor software errors, non-critical device down
P4 Low Minor issue, routine request, question, or enhancement. No immediate business impact. Can be scheduled. Password reset, software installation, training request, feature questions, scheduled maintenance, cable management
Priority Assignment: Tech & Electrical Services LLC reserves the right to adjust priority classification based on technical assessment. Clients may request a review of priority assignment within 1 hour of ticket creation.

Exclusions

Important: The following events and conditions are excluded from SLA calculations. Downtime or response delays caused by excluded events will not trigger SLA credits.

SLA Clock Exclusions

  • Scheduled Maintenance: Planned maintenance during approved windows (Sundays 2 AM–6 AM PST) with 72-hour advance notice provided to client.
  • Client-Caused Delays: Client fails to provide timely access, credentials, approvals, or information required to proceed with resolution.
  • Client-Initiated Changes: Client makes unauthorized changes to systems, configurations, or infrastructure that directly cause or contribute to the incident.
  • Force Majeure: Natural disasters, earthquakes, floods, fires, pandemics, acts of war, civil unrest, or other events beyond reasonable control.
  • Third-Party Service Failures: Outages or failures by ISPs, cloud providers (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud), telecom carriers, utility companies, or other third parties not under TES management.
  • Internet / Utility Outages: Loss of internet connectivity or electrical power at client site due to ISP/utility provider failure.
  • Hardware Failure Outside Scope: Failure of client-owned hardware not under a TES hardware management contract or outside the covered hardware list.
  • Software Vendor Issues: Bugs, outages, or degraded service caused by third-party software vendors (Microsoft, Adobe, QuickBooks, etc.).
  • Security Incidents Caused by Client: Security breaches, data loss, or malware infections resulting from client failure to implement recommended security controls.
  • Non-Covered Locations: Sites beyond the agreed service area (30-mile radius from Temecula) unless a separate on-site SLA has been negotiated.
  • Out-of-Scope Work: Requests for services not covered under the current MSA or SOW without an approved change order.
  • Emergency Maintenance: Urgent maintenance required to prevent imminent security risk or catastrophic failure; client notified with minimum 2-hour notice when feasible.
  • Waived Credits: Credits are voided if client has any outstanding invoices past 30 days due at time of SLA breach.

Emergency After-Hours Procedures

⚠ After-Hours Emergency Support is reserved for P1/P2 incidents only. Non-emergency contacts outside business hours will be addressed the next business day. Repeated non-emergency after-hours contacts may result in a policy review.

Qualifying After-Hours Emergencies

Qualifies as Emergency Does NOT Qualify
✅ Complete network or server outage ❌ Password resets
✅ Active cybersecurity breach or ransomware ❌ Software installation requests
✅ POS / payment system failure ❌ Performance complaints (non-critical)
✅ Data loss or active exfiltration detected ❌ Feature requests or questions
✅ Electrical fault causing immediate safety risk ❌ Scheduled tasks that could wait
✅ Fire suppression / UPS / power failure in server room ❌ Printer / peripheral issues
✅ Physical security breach (cameras down, door access failure) ❌ Non-critical device failures

After-Hours Escalation Procedure

1

Attempt Self-Service First: Check the TES Client Portal for known outages, maintenance notices, or status updates. Confirm the issue is production-impacting before escalating.

2

Submit an Emergency Ticket: Log into the Client Portal and mark ticket priority as P1 or P2. Include: description, number of affected users, business impact, and any error messages.

3

Call the Emergency Hotline (Gold/Platinum only): Call the dedicated after-hours emergency number. Have your client account number and ticket number ready. This line is monitored 24/7 by on-call staff.

4

Silver/Bronze After-Hours: Submit the portal ticket. On-call technician will review P1/P2 tickets within 60–120 minutes. After-hours surcharge fees apply (see Section 7).

5

Electrical Emergencies: For life-safety electrical emergencies (sparks, burning smell, tripped main breaker, flooding near electrical), call 911 first. Then contact the TES emergency line. Do NOT re-energize circuits without authorization from a licensed electrician.

6

Await Confirmation: On-call technician will acknowledge within SLA windows and provide an estimated resolution time or on-site arrival window. Maintain communication until resolved.
