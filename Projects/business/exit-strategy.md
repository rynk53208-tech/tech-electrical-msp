## Executive Summary

This document outlines exit strategy options for Irvin's tech and electrical services business. The goal is to build maximum sellable value while maintaining optionality—whether that means a full exit, partial sale, or succession to family.

**Key Takeaway:** At current trajectory (replacing $114K salary), Irvin's business could be worth **$150K–$400K+** if properly structured. The window to exit is typically when MRR hits $8K–$12K/month with 3+ years of growth history.

---

## 1. Business Valuation

### Current State
- **Target:** Replace $114K/year salary (~$9.5K/month)
- **Current MRR:** Not yet at target (baseline viable at 10 clients)
- **Services:** Cybersecurity, software dev, MSP/MSSP, computer repair (including micro-soldering), electrical services

### Valuation Benchmarks (IT/MSP Industry)

| Business Type | Typical Multiple | Notes |
|---------------|------------------|-------|
| **MSP (recurring revenue)** | 2x–5x ARR | Higher with long-term contracts |
| **Break-fix / repair** | 0.5x–1.5x revenue | Lower multiples, less predictable |
| **Cybersecurity niche** | 3x–7x ARR | Premium for specialization |
| **Consulting / dev** | 1x–3x revenue | Depends on client concentration |

### Estimated Value for Irvin's Business

**Scenario A: Early Stage (Baseline)**
- MRR: $5,000/month | ARR: $60,000
- Multiple: 2x–3x
- **Value: $120K–$180K**
- 10 clients, service-heavy, limited recurring

**Scenario B: Salary Replacement**
- MRR: $9,500/month | ARR: $114,000
- Multiple: 3x–4x
- **Value: $340K–$455K**
- 15+ clients, mixed recurring + projects

**Scenario C: Growth Mode**
- MRR: $15,000/month | ARR: $180,000
- Multiple: 3.5x–5x
- **Value: $630K–$900K**
- 20+ clients, strong recurring, niche differentiation (cyber + electrical)

### What Drives Valuation

**High Value (multipliers up):**
- ✓ Long-term contracts (2–3 years)
- ✓ High MRR percentage (60%+ of revenue)
- ✓ Diversified client base (no single client >20%)
- ✓ Certified staff / technical differentiation
- ✓ Recurring revenue streams (monitoring, RMM)
- ✓ Unique niche: cyber + electrical combo is rare
- ✓ Documented processes and playbooks

**Low Value (multipliers down):**
- ✗ Heavy reliance on owner (no key employees)
- ✗ Single client concentration
- ✗ Break-fix focus (no contracts)
- ✗ Client churn >15%/year
- ✗ No documented procedures

## 2. How to Build Sellable Value

### Priority Actions (Next 12–24 Months)

#### A. Maximize Recurring Revenue
- **Target:** 60%+ of revenue from retainers/MSSP contracts
- Shift clients from hourly to retainer models
- Add RMM/monitoring as mandatory add-on
- Offer annual contract discounts (improves valuation)

#### B. Diversify Client Base
- No client >20% of revenue
- Target: 15–25 active clients
- Mix: 60% SMB, 25% residential, 15% enterprise

#### C. Build Institutional Knowledge
- Document everything: SOPs, runbooks, onboarding
- Use PSA (Professional Services Automation) tool
- Track metrics: SLAs, response times, client satisfaction
- Create client onboarding playbooks

#### D. Develop Key Personnel
- Hire/develop 1–2 technicians who can run operations
- Reduce owner dependency
- Buyers pay premium for "business in a box"

#### E. Specialize and Differentiate
- Lean into cyber + electrical combo (unique selling point)
- Get certifications: CompTIA, Cisco, Microsoft, electrical
- Position as "full-stack infrastructure" (IT + electrical)
- Build case studies / testimonials

#### F. Clean Financials
- Separate business/personal expenses
- Show consistent 12–24 month growth
- Maximize profitability (buyers value margin %)
- Have clean P&L ready for due diligence

## 3. Exit Options

### Option A: Sell to Strategic Buyer (MSP/IT Company)

**Pros:**
- Highest multiple (3x–5x ARR)
- They can immediately integrate and grow
- Often pay cash at close
- Fastest path (2–4 months)

**Cons:**
- May consolidate/rename your business
- Client retention risk (10–20% churn typical)
- Usually requires 2–3 year earnout
- Less control post-sale

**Best for:** When you want out completely, business is running well, and you have 2+ years to prepare.

**Valuation:** $300K–$900K (at growth stage)

---

### Option B: Sell to Private Equity / Aggregator

**Pros:**
- Professional process, all cash sometimes
- Often keep existing brand/team
- Roll-up opportunities (you stay on as manager)

**Cons:**
- Lower multiples than strategic (2.5x–4x)
- Heavy due diligence
- May require equity rollover
- They want scale, not niche

**Best for:** Business at $500K+ ARR, ready to scale further, willing to stay 2–3 years.

**Valuation:** $300K–$600K (at growth stage)

---

### Option C: Merge with Complementary Business

**Pros:**
- Combine strengths (e.g., another MSP, web dev shop)
- Share costs, cross-refer clients
- May keep equity and role
- Less disruptive than full sale

**Cons:**
- Requires finding right partner
- Cultural fit issues
- Slower process
- Negotiations can stall

**Best for:** Irvin has unique electrical component—merge with pure-play MSP could be powerful.

**Valuation:** Depends on structure (50/50 split typically values each at 1.5x–2x contribution)

---

### Option D: Pass to Family

**Pros:**
- Keep legacy in family
- Potentially lower tax implications
- Flexible terms (installment payments)
- Emotional satisfaction

**Cons:**
- Family may not have technical skills
- Need to hire/manage non-owner
- Slower ROI than sale
- Requires 3–5 year transition

**Best for:** If Irvin has family members interested in tech, or wants to slow down gradually.

**Consider:**
- Create a vesting schedule (2–5 years)
- Hire professional manager if family isn't technical
- Set clear buyout terms

---

### Option E: IPO / Venture (Unlikely)

**Pros:**
- Maximum value
- Public company status

**Cons:**
- Not realistic for MSP at this scale
- Years of growth needed
- Massive regulatory burden

**Verdict:** Not a realistic option for this business model.

## 4. When to Exit

### Timing Indicators (Green Lights)

| Metric | Exit-Ready Threshold |
|--------|---------------------|
| MRR | $8,000–$12,000/month |
| Client count | 15+ active clients |
| Revenue growth | 20%+ YoY for 2+ years |
| Client retention | >85% annual |
| Owner independence | Can step away 2–4 weeks |
| Team | 2+ capable technicians |

### Best Exit Windows

**Short-term (1–2 years):**
- Only if personal circumstances require it
- Will accept lower multiple (1.5x–2.5x)
- Likely strategic buyer for "talent + client base"

**Medium-term (2–4 years):**
- Optimal window at $8K–$12K MRR
- Multiple: 3x–4x
- Growth history makes business attractive

**Long-term (4–7 years):**
- Peak value at $15K+ MRR, 20+ clients
- Multiple: 4x–5x
- Can pick best buyer, negotiate terms

### Risk Factors (When NOT to Exit)

- Market downturn (valuations drop 20–40%)
- Key client just left (wait for stability)
- Major technical debt (fix operations first)
- No documented processes (buyer red flag)
- Owner burnout (buyers notice)

## 5. Recommended Path

### 18-Month Roadmap

| Quarter | Focus | Exit Readiness Impact |
|---------|-------|------------------------|
| Q1 | Push MRR to $6K, get to 10 clients | Foundation |
| Q2 | Document SOPs, hire first tech | Reduces owner dependency |
| Q3 | Push MRR to $8K, add 5 more clients | Baseline exit-ready |
| Q4 | Clean financials, formalize metrics | Due diligence ready |
| Q5–Q6 | Scale to $10K+ MRR, optimize operations | Peak exit value |

### Decision Framework

**If personal need arises (health, career change, relocation):**
- Sell at whatever stage you're at
- Prioritize clean exit over max value

**If business is humming and offers come:**
- Don't exit below $250K valuation
- Negotiate earnout terms (stay 1–2 years)

**If no urgency:**
- Build to $12K MRR minimum
- Wait for strategic buyer at 3.5x+
- Consider merging with complementary biz
