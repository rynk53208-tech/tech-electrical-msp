# Proposal Follow-Up Script
## For Irvin's Tech & Electrical Services Business

---

## 1. When to Follow Up

### Timeline
| Touchpoint | Timing | Purpose |
|------------|--------|---------|
| **Initial Follow-Up** | 3-5 days after proposal sent | Confirm receipt, answer questions |
| **Second Touch** | 7-10 days after first follow-up | Address any stalls, provide more info |
| **Third Touch** | 14-21 days after second | Final check-in, re-engage interest |
| **Nurture** | If no response after 3 attempts | Add to warm list, check in quarterly |

### Trigger-Based Follow-Ups
- **Decision-maker changed roles** — Congratulate, reintroduce
- **Company news (funding, expansion, new hire)** — Relevant service opportunity
- **Industry news** — Position your expertise as relevant
- **Past client referral mention** — Leverage social proof
- **Their public project launch** — Congratulate + "I can help with..."

## 2. What to Say

### Email Templates

**Subject: Quick question on [Project Name] proposal**

> Hi [Name],
> 
> Hoping you got my proposal for [service] — wanted to make sure it didn't get lost in your inbox.
> 
> A few clients have asked about [related topic], which made me think of your project. Any questions I can answer?
> 
> Quick yes/no: Is this still a priority for Q[/current quarter]?
> 
> Either way, happy to help when the time is right.
> 
> — Irvin

**Subject: Following up — [Service] for [Company]**

> [Name],
> 
> Just circling back on the proposal I sent [date]. I know you're busy — just want to make sure you have everything you need to move forward.
> 
> If the timing's off, no worries. If it makes sense, I'm happy to:
> - Hop on a 10-min call
> - Refine the scope
> - Provide references from similar projects
> 
> Let me know either way so I can plan accordingly.
> 
> Best,  
> Irvin

### Phone Script (Cold/Warm)
> "Hey [Name], it's Irvin. I sent over a proposal a few days ago and wanted to see if you had any questions. I know you're swamped, so I can make this quick — just wanted to make sure we didn't miss anything. Can I help move this forward?"

## 3. Handling Objections

### "We're still evaluating options"

> "Totally get it. When you say 'evaluating,' are you comparing [specific service] against in-house, or looking at other vendors? That way I can make sure my proposal is hitting the right points."

**Pivot:** Ask what "done" looks like for them. Timeline, decision criteria, who's involved.

### "Budget is tight right now"

> "I hear you — everyone's being careful with spend. The question is whether this is a 'wait and see' or a 'cut entirely.' Because what I find is, the cost of *not* solving [problem] usually ends up being higher than the investment to fix it. Where are you seeing the biggest impact?"

**Pivot:** Focus on ROI, phased approach, or priority projects.

### "We went with another vendor"

> "No problem — who did you go with? [Listen.] That's a solid choice. If things don't work out, I'd welcome the chance to be your backup. Can I check in in 90 days?"

**Pivot:** Plant seed for future. Ask what they went with and why (learning).

### "Send me more information"

> "Absolutely — what specifically would help? I can tailor what I'm sending to address [their specific concern]. Are there any particular pain points you want to make sure we cover?"

**Pivot:** Get specific. Don't just send generic info.

### "I'm not the decision-maker"

> "Thanks for the heads up. Who's driving this decision? And is there a good time to loop them in? Happy to schedule something where we can all align."

**Pivot:** Get name, schedule multi-party call.

### "Not a good time / Call me back later"

> "For sure. What's a better window? I'll put a reminder in my calendar so I don't miss it."

**Pivot:** Get commitment. If they deflect again, email is better.

## 4. Closing Techniques

### The Soft Close
> "Does this make sense so far? Any concerns before I send the final agreement?"

### The Two-Choice Close
> "Would you prefer to start with the security audit first, or the full implementation? Either way, I can have the team ready for [date]."

### The Summary Close
> "Just to recap: We deliver [X], you get [Y outcome], and the investment is [Z]. Do we have alignment to move forward?"

### The Scarcity/Urgency Close (use sparingly)
> "I have availability the week of [date], but it's filling up. If we sign by [date], we can hit your Q2 deadline."

### The Next-Step Close
> "Great — let's get the paperwork over to you. Should I send the agreement to you or your finance contact?"

### The Assumption Close
> "I'll draft the SOW and have it to you by tomorrow. Does Thursday work for a kickoff call?"

---

## Key Principles

1. **Lead with value, not desperation** — You're offering help, not begging for work
2. **Make it easy to say yes** — Reduce friction, be flexible on timing
3. **Never leave a voicemail without a next step** — "I'll follow up via email"
4. **Follow up on your follow-ups** — Consistency = credibility
5. **Track everything** — Notes in CRM, next actions, last contact date
6. **Respect the "no"** — If they say no twice, give space but keep warm
