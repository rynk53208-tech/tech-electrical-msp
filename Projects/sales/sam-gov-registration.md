## Prerequisites

Before you register on SAM.gov, gather these essentials:

### 1. **UEI (Unique Entity Identifier)**
- **What it is:** A 12-character identifier assigned by SAMDBU (System for Award Management Data Build Utility)
- **How to get it:** Register at [sam.gov](https://www.sam.gov) or apply at [entity.samdbu.org](https://entity.samdbu.org)
- **Timeline:** 1-3 days, though can be faster
- **Cost:** Free
- **Why:** Required by federal agencies; replaces the DUNS number (though DUNS still matters for some contexts)
- **Action:** Get this FIRST—everything else depends on it

### 2. **Tax ID (TIN) or FEIN**
- **What it is:** Your Employer Identification Number (EIN) or Social Security Number (SSN) if sole proprietor
- **For 1-person businesses:** You can use your SSN or an EIN (form SS-4)
- **Recommendation:** Get an EIN (free, IRS.gov) even as a solo operator—it separates personal from business taxes and looks more professional
- **Timeline:** Immediate if online
- **Have ready:** 
  - Legal business name
  - Business address (real office or home office is fine)
  - Phone number
  - Date business started

### 3. **Business Bank Account**
- **Why needed:** To verify legitimacy and receive payments
- **What to bring:** EIN letter, business license, ID
- **Recommendation:** Open before SAM registration (shows you're serious)
- **Cost:** Most banks offer free business checking

### 4. **Business License & Registration**
- **State level:** Register with California Secretary of State
- **Local level:** Get a California Seller's Permit (if applicable) and local business license
- **Timeline:** 1-2 weeks via online filing
- **Cost:** $50-150 depending on structure

### 5. **CAGE Code (Optional but Recommended)**
- **What it is:** Commercial and Government Entity code
- **How to get it:** Assigned when you register on SAM.gov (automatic)
- **Why it matters:** Required for some contracts; agencies use it for vendor lookups

### 6. **DUNS Number (Still Useful)**
- **What it is:** D-U-N-S Number from Dun & Bradstreet
- **How to get it:** Free at [dnb.com](https://www.dnb.com)
- **Why:** Some contractors and subcontractors still request it; good to have on file
- **Timeline:** 24 hours

### 7. **Documentation Ready**
Scan and have digital copies of:
- Business license
- EIN letter
- Bank account statement (current)
- Proof of address (utility bill or lease)
- Your government-issued ID (driver's license)

## Step-by-Step Registration

### Phase 1: Pre-Registration (1-2 Days)

#### Step 1: Get Your UEI
1. Go to [sam.gov](https://www.sam.gov)
2. Click **"Create Login"** (if no account)
3. Enter email and create password
4. Verify email address
5. During account creation, if no UEI exists, select **"Register for a new UEI"**
6. Fill in business information (legal name, address, business type)
7. SAM will assign a UEI and confirm via email
8. **Note the UEI** — you'll need this constantly

#### Step 2: Verify You're an FPDS Registrant (Optional but Recommended)
- This allows you to be visible in the Federal Procurement Data System
- Most IT/MSP businesses should register; it increases visibility
- Can be set up during main SAM registration

---

### Phase 2: SAM.gov Full Registration (3-5 Days)

#### Step 3: Log Into SAM and Start Entity Registration
1. Log into your SAM account
2. Navigate to **"Workspace"** → **"Entity Information"**
3. Start the **"Entity Registration"** workflow

#### Step 4: Business Information Section
**Legal Business Name:**
- Enter exactly as it appears on your business license
- Example: *"Irvin Avitia Tech Services, LLC"* or *"Tech Electrical & Cyber Solutions"*

**Business Type:**
- Select **"Small Business"** (most likely)
- Specify sub-category (e.g., "Information Technology Services" or "Specialized IT Services")

**Address:**
- Physical address (home office is acceptable)
- Mailing address (can be same)
- Headquarters address

**Principal Place of Business:**
- Street address of where you actually work
- City: Your CA city
- State: California
- ZIP: Your ZIP

**UEI:**
- Pre-filled from Step 1

**TIN/FEIN:**
- Enter your business EIN (recommended) or SSN

#### Step 5: Points of Contact (POC)
You'll need to assign:
- **Authorized Organization Representative (AOR)**
  - Usually you (for 1-person business)
  - Your name, email, phone
- **Government Business POC**
  - Can be same as AOR
  - This is who contracting officers contact
- **Technical POC** (Optional)
  - For technical inquiries about your services

**For each POC:**
- Full legal name
- Title (e.g., "Owner" or "Principal")
- Email address (use professional domain if possible)
- Phone number
- Fax (optional)

#### Step 6: Business Information Details
**Nature of Business:**
- Select NAICS codes relevant to your services:
  - **541519** — Other computer related services
  - **541511** — Custom computer programming services
  - **561210** — Facilities support services (if offering managed services)
  - **541715** — Research & development in electrical/electronic equipment
  - Add 1-3 most relevant NAICS codes

**Annual Revenue:**
- Estimate conservatively (can update annually)
- SAM uses this for small business contracting calculations
- Don't inflate; honesty matters

**Number of Employees:**
- If solo: Enter "1"
- Don't include contractors/1099s unless they're on payroll

**Years in Business:**
- Enter when you started operations

#### Step 7: Certifications & Statuses
This is CRITICAL. Check applicable boxes:

**Mandatory certifications (if applicable):**
- ☐ Veteran-Owned Small Business (VOSB) — if veteran-owned
- ☐ Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business (SDVOSB) — if you have service-connected disability
- ☐ Woman-Owned Small Business (WOSB) — if women-owned (51%+)
- ☐ Historically Underutilized Business Zone (HUBZone)
- ☐ 8(a) Business Development Program (if eligible; see certification section)
- ☐ Minority-Owned Business Enterprise (MBE)
- ☐ Disadvantaged Small Business

**Check only if TRUE and you have documentation.** False claims = debarment.

#### Step 8: Banking Information
- Bank account name
- Bank routing number
- Bank account number
- Account type (checking)
- **Flag as Primary Banking Account** (checkmark)

**Security Note:** This is encrypted. SAM uses it for ACH payments.

#### Step 9: Performance Data (Optional)
- Prior government contracts? (Yes/No)
- Links to relevant past performance
- You can skip if no prior work; update after first contract

#### Step 10: Federal Tax Registration
- Confirm TIN on file
- Select **"Authorized to conduct business as U.S. contractor"**

#### Step 11: Representations & Certifications (R&Cs)
You'll be asked to certify:
- Not debarred or suspended from federal contracting
- Not owned by a foreign entity
- Comply with federal employment laws
- Have worker's compensation insurance (or exemption)
- Understand regulations about export controls

**Read carefully.** Incorrect certifications = contract cancellation.

#### Step 12: Legal Business Structure
Confirm your entity type:
- Sole proprietorship
- LLC (most common for small IT shops)
- S-Corp
- C-Corp
- Partnership

#### Step 13: Review & Submit
- Review all entered data
- Correct any errors
- **Submit for SAM review**

---

### Phase 3: Verification & Activation (3-7 Days)

#### Step 14: Wait for Initial Verification
- SAM reviews your submission
- You'll receive email confirmation of receipt
- UEI becomes **ACTIVE** once approved
- Timeline: 1-3 business days typically

#### Step 15: Annual Renewal Setup
- After approval, you must **renew annually** (usually before your registration anniversary)
- Calendar this: SAM sends reminders
- Renewal only takes 10-15 minutes if no changes
- **If you miss renewal deadline, your UEI goes inactive** (you can reactivate)

## Key Certifications

### 1. **8(a) Business Development Program**

**What it is:**
- SBA-run program for economically disadvantaged small business owners
- Provides:
  - Set-asides on federal contracts (only 8(a) firms can bid)
  - Technical assistance & mentoring
  - Business development support
  - Potentially higher margins (less competition)

**Eligibility (generally):**
- U.S. citizen owner (51%+ ownership)
- Personally economically disadvantaged (net worth <$250k typically, not including primary residence)
- Business economically disadvantaged (average annual revenue <$13.5M, varies by industry)
- In business 2+ years (exceptions exist)

**Benefits:**
- Automatic small business size standard eligibility in many categories
- Access to federal set-asides (potentially higher contract values)
- SBA mentoring & training

**Timeline to Get It:**
- 4-6 weeks
- Apply at [sba.gov/8a](https://www.sba.gov/8a)
- Will need: business plan, financial statements, personal financial statement

**Cost:** Free

**For a 1-person IT shop:** If you're economically disadvantaged (most solo operators are when starting), this is WORTH IT. The set-asides alone make it valuable.

---

### 2. **Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business (SDVOSB)**

**What it is:**
- Reserved contracts for veterans with service-connected disabilities
- Only SDVOSB-verified firms can bid
- Often less competition = easier wins

**Eligibility:**
- Veteran with service-connected disability rating (VA)
- 51%+ ownership of business
- Substantially involved in daily operations (you must be working)

**Benefits:**
- Contracting set-asides
- Contracting priority with federal agencies
- Some agencies have SDVOSB procurement goals (they MUST award to SDVOSBs)

**Timeline to Get It:**
- Verify via VetBiz (vetbiz.sba.gov)
- 2-3 weeks once in SAM
- Need: DD-214, VA disability rating letter

**Cost:** Free

**For Irvin:** If applicable, this is gold. Government contracting officers HAVE TO reserve money for SDVOSBs. Huge advantage.

---

### 3. **Woman-Owned Small Business (WOSB) / Women-Owned in Traditionally Underrepresented Sector (WOTUS)**

**What it is:**
- Reserved set-asides for businesses 51%+ owned/controlled by women
- Women must be working in business

**Eligibility:**
- Women own 51%+ of the business
- Women are involved in daily operations
- U.S. citizen

**Benefits:**
- Set-asides on contracts
- Federal procurement goals (agencies must spend % with WOSBs)

**Timeline to Get It:**
- Verify via https://www.certify.sba.gov/
- 10 business days

**Cost:** Free

---

### 4. **HUBZone (Historically Underutilized Business Zone)**

**What it is:**
- Contracts set-aside for small businesses in economically distressed areas
- Your primary place of business + 35% of employees must be in a HUBZone

**Eligibility:**
- Business located in HUBZone (check: [hubzone.sba.gov](https://www.hubzone.sba.gov))
- 51%+ owner is U.S. citizen
- At least 35% of employees live in HUBZone

**Temecula, CA:** Check the HUBZone map. Temecula is in Riverside County (mixed coverage). May or may not qualify.

**Benefits:**
- Set-asides
- Price evaluation adjustments (10% preference on some contracts)

**Timeline to Get It:**
- Verify via SBA HUBZone program
- 2-3 weeks

**Cost:** Free

---

### 5. **Minority-Owned Business Enterprise (MBE)**

**What it is:**
- Businesses owned by minorities (African-American, Hispanic, Asian-Pacific, Native American, etc.)
- Some contracts set-aside for MBEs

**Eligibility:**
- 51%+ ownership by qualifying minority members
- Involved in operations

**Benefits:**
- Set-asides on some contracts
- Better access to subcontracting opportunities

**Timeline:** Varies by certifier; via SBA or state certifying partners (2-4 weeks)

**Cost:** Usually free from SBA; $300-500 if going through private certifier

---

### Certification Strategy for Your 1-Person IT Shop

**Priority ranking (if applicable):**

1. **SDVOSB** (if service-disabled veteran) — Most valuable in gov contracting
2. **8(a)** (if economically disadvantaged) — Huge set-aside value
3. **HUBZone** (if in qualified zone) — Check first
4. **WOSB** (if woman-owned) — Good access to set-asides
5. **MBE** (if minority-owned) — Good but less contracts set-aside than others

**Action:** Start with SAM registration first. Then apply for certifications you genuinely qualify for. Each certification takes 2-4 weeks but is FREE and opens contract opportunities.

## GSA Schedule Basics

### What is the GSA Schedule?

**GSA (General Services Administration)** operates the **GSA Schedule** — a pre-approved list of vendors authorized to sell services to federal agencies **without formal bidding**.

**Key points:**
- Pre-negotiated rates (you set margins, GSA negotiates)
- "Multiple Award Schedule" (MAS) — multiple vendors per category
- Agencies use it for convenience/speed
- No competitive bidding required for most Schedule orders
- Orders can range from $5K to $1M+

---

### GSA Schedule for IT Services

**Relevant schedules for IT/MSP:**

| Schedule Code | Name | What It Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **84 (IT Schedule)** | Information Technology | Custom development, consulting, systems integration, helpdesk, managed services |
| **246** | Facility Maintenance | Includes some IT infrastructure if bundled with facilities |
| **871** | Security Systems Integration | Cybersecurity, physical security, integration |

**For a 1-person IT/MSP shop:** GSA Schedule 84 (IT Services) is your target.

---

### GSA Schedule Registration Process

#### Step 1: Determine Your Service Category
GSA uses **PSC (Product/Service Code)** codes. For IT:

| PSC | Category |
|---|---|
| **D311** | Software development (custom) |
| **D316** | IT consulting & systems integration |
| **D319** | IT training & documentation |
| **D321** | Help desk/user support services |
| **D322** | Managed services / IT managed support |
| **D323** | Cybersecurity services |
| **D325** | Cloud computing services |

**For Irvin (IT + electrical):** Likely D316 (IT consulting), D322 (managed services), and D323 (cybersecurity).

#### Step 2: Prepare Pricing Strategy
- **Commercial Best Pricing (CBP):** You must offer government at least as good as commercial customers
- **Discount tiers:** Common to tier by quantity or annual volume
- **Labor rates:** For time-and-materials, set hourly rates
  - Junior tech: $85-120/hr
  - Senior tech: $130-200/hr
  - Consultants: $150-300+/hr
  - Cybersecurity specialist: $200-400+/hr
  - Scale up based on your experience (Northrop-level = premium rates)

**Pro tip:** Set rates slightly higher than you'd bid directly; agencies expect to pay a premium for Schedule convenience.

#### Step 3: Gather Documents for GSA
- **Technical proposal:** What services you offer, capabilities, past performance
- **Pricing proposal:** Labor rates, service fees, minimums
- **Compliance documents:** 
  - Insurance (E&O liability, general liability)
  - Quality control plan
  - Past performance references (if available; can start with 0)

#### Step 4: Submit via eOffer
- Log into [eOffer.gsa.gov](https://eoffer.gsa.gov)
- Create vendor profile
- Submit technical & pricing proposals
- GSA reviews (typically 30-90 days)

#### Step 5: Negotiate & Award
- GSA may request revisions
- You'll negotiate rates if needed
- Once agreed, you're added to the Schedule
- Agencies can now order from you

---

### Timeline & Costs

| Step | Timeline | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| eOffer submission | Varies | Free |
| Initial review | 2-4 weeks | — |
| Potential revisions | 1-2 weeks (back & forth) | — |
| Final negotiation | 1-3 weeks | — |
| Award notice | 30-90 days total | Free |

**Total:** 2-6 months typical. Start immediately if pursuing GSA.

---

### GSA Schedule Revenue Potential

**Why it matters:**

- **Volume:** Agencies LOVE using GSA; you can get steady orders
- **Less competition:** Only approved vendors; no constant bidding
- **Recurring revenue:** Many agencies renew GSA orders annually
- **Visibility:** Listed in GSA eBuy, searchable by agencies

**Reality:**
- First year: Typically 2-5 orders (maybe $10K-50K)
- Year 2+: Can scale to $100K-500K+ if you market it
- Success depends on: pricing competitiveness, past performance, active marketing

## Tips for Winning Government Contracts

### 1. **Know Your Competitive Advantage**
**You have (Irvin):**
- Northrop Grumman background (cybersecurity + DevOps credibility)
- Rare combo: IT + electrical + board-level repair
- MSP/MSSP experience
- Small = agile, responsive, personal service

**Pitch this hard.** Government agencies want security expertise. Your Northrop pedigree is GOLD.

### 2. **Market to Niches, Not Broad Strokes**

**Instead of:** "IT services for anyone"
**Say:** "Cybersecurity compliance & incident response for federal MSP programs" or "Mission-critical systems hardening (IT + electrical integration)"

Example pitch:
> "Specialized in federal security compliance for critical infrastructure. Northrop Grumman-trained cyber engineer + rare hardware expertise (board-level repair, electrical systems). Ideal for agencies needing secure, resilient MSP services."

### 3. **Build Past Performance Early**

**Challenge:** You're competing against vendors with 10-year Track Records.

**Solution:**
- **Start with small contracts** (even if lower margin)
  - $5K-$15K first gigs (easier to win)
  - Deliver flawlessly (on time, under budget, excellent QA)
- **Get testimonials** (even if client is small)
  - "Completed security audit on schedule, identified 47 critical vulnerabilities"
  - "MSP services scaled from 3 agencies to 6 with 99.8% uptime"
- **Case studies** (sanitized if needed for NDA)
  - Describe problem → solution → result
  - Include metrics (% improvement, cost savings, uptime, security posture)

**Pro tip:** Highlight FIRST federal contracts prominently. Agencies want new vendors but need confidence.

### 4. **Price Competitively but Profitably**

**Government contracting pricing rules:**
- Must offer best pricing available to any customer (CBP)
- But can tier by volume, effort, or contract type
- Small contract (< $50K) = often higher margin % to offset admin cost
- Large contract (> $100K) = lower margin but higher revenue

**Strategy:**
- Labor rates: $120-180/hr (adjust by specialty)
- Markup on hardware: 15-25%
- Travel: Bill hourly + mileage + per diem
- Support: Flat monthly or per-incident

### 5. **Master the Proposal Process**

**Winning proposals have:**

1. **Executive Summary (1 page max)**
   - What problem you're solving
   - Why you're the best fit
   - Key differentiator (your Northrop background + technical depth)

2. **Understanding of Requirements**
   - Restate the agency's needs in YOUR words
   - Show you "get it"

3. **Technical Approach**
   - Step-by-step how you'll deliver
   - Realistic timeline
   - Quality assurance plan
   - Risk mitigation

4. **Team/Personnel**
   - Your background (even as 1-person, emphasize depth)
   - Key subcontractors if needed (partner with other IT firms for overflow)
   - Certifications, clearances, training

5. **Past Performance**
   - 3-5 relevant examples
   - Start with non-government if needed ("Provided cybersecurity audit for 15-person biotech firm; identified 52 critical issues, 100% remediated")
   - Include testimonials/references

6. **Pricing**
   - Clear labor rates
   - Hardware/software costs itemized
   - Travel/admin if applicable
   - Any volume discounts

7. **Schedule**
   - Gantt chart or timeline
   - Milestones & deliverables
   - Contingency buffer (+10-15%)

### 6. **Use Small Business Advantages**

**Agencies have goals:**
- Spend X% with small businesses
- Spend Y% with women-owned, veteran-owned, 8(a), etc.

**You:**
- Are small (huge advantage)
- Can get additional certifications (SDVOSB, 8(a), HUBZone, etc.)
- Are responsive (no 6-week approval chains)

**Pitch these:** "As a certified small business (if applicable: SDVOSB/8(a)/HUBZone), we meet your procurement goals while delivering expert-level service."

### 7. **Build Relationships With Contracting Officers**

**Government buys from people they know.**

- **Attend industry events:** FedTradeLink, ProNet conferences, SBA webinars
- **LinkedIn:** Connect with agency IT directors, contracting officers, program managers
- **Cold outreach:** "Hi [officer], I noticed your agency uses IT managed services. We specialize in federal compliance (Northrop-trained). Would you be open to a brief call?"
- **Networking:** Chamber of commerce, local SBA chapter, tech meetups

**Reality:** 60% of government contracts go to vendors already in an agency's network.

### 8. **Track Contracts & Bid Systematically**

**Don't just react.** Be proactive.

- **Set up alerts:**
  - SAM.gov for your contract categories
  - FedBizOpps.gov searches
  - LinkedIn job postings from agencies you target
- **Track competition:** See who's winning what
- **Bid 2-3x per month minimum** (even small wins build portfolio)
- **Win rate goal:** Aim for 20-30% win rate (1 win per 3-5 bids is healthy)

### 9. **Compliance & Certifications Matter**

**Before bidding, ensure:**
- ☐ SAM registration is current (renew annually)
- ☐ All claimed certifications are valid (8(a), HUBZone, etc.)
- ☐ Insurance current (E&O, general liability)
- ☐ No compliance issues (tax liens, debarment checks)
- ☐ Past performance references ready to provide

**Red flags agencies check:**
- Debarred vendors → instant disqualification
- Lapsed insurance → bid rejected
- Inaccurate certifications → contract cancellation + possible prosecution

### 10. **Start Small, Think Big**

**Your first contracts will likely be:**
- Cybersecurity audits ($5K-$20K)
- Incident response consulting ($100-300/hr)
- MSP services (monthly retainer $2K-$10K)
- Hardware/board repair ($500-$5K per job)

**From there:**
- Build case studies
- Win larger contracts ($50K-$200K)
- Get GSA Schedule (recurring revenue)
- Subcontract for bigger vendors (they'll hire you for overflow)

**2-year goal:** $200K-500K annual government revenue (realistic for 1-person shop with strong pitch).

## Final Notes

**Government contracting is not get-rich-quick, but it's reliable.**

- **Pros:** Stable funding, repeat customers, creditworthy (payment always on time), often multi-year contracts, your Northrop background is a huge asset
- **Cons:** Slower sales cycles (3-6 months from bid to contract), compliance burden, lower margins than commercial (government expects discounts)
- **Break-even:** Usually 3-6 months of effort before first win

**Key to success:**
1. Get registered (SAM.gov) immediately
2. Get certifications that apply (SDVOSB/8(a) are GOLD if eligible)
3. Build GSA Schedule early (passive revenue)
4. Bid consistently (don't give up after 2-3 rejections)
5. Deliver flawlessly on first contracts (reputation = future wins)
6. Network (half of government contracting is relationships)

**Your secret weapon:** Northrop Grumman background + rare IT + electrical + board-level repair combo. That's a niche market in government contracting. Own it.
